I won't happy with this guide because it did not tell about best solution for Android users. Android owner could have a full Android Auto experience with AIO Tweaks at no cost in few seconds: no wrecking, no upgrade, no spare parts, no fv 70, just a working Android Auto (usb + wireless) with a simple firmware hack on original firmware (v56). And you get also touch screen with Maps and Waze: what a wonderful world.
If you followed this guide and you installed Android Auto, you are f*cked because you can't have Android Auto wireless unless you do a software and hardware downgrade: no way! I'm one of you, I want Android Auto wireless but I followed this guide.

Now, there is a good solution for Android users. It's AAWireless, you can order it on Indiegogo and wait 3 - 5 months for shipping. I did my order in February and I got it in May.
The point is: AAWireless works! And works well! It's really simple, you install AAWireless Config app on you Android smartphone, connect AAWireless box to usb port in our car, do the first setup and ... done! You got Android Auto wireless perfectly working. The AAWireless box works as a "gateway", so your smartphone connects via wifi with AAWireless box. The connection time is incredibly fast, no difference with cabled Android Auto. All works fine until now. I tested a lot navigation (Maps and Waze) while playing Spotify: no glitch, no sound interruption, no problems. I have to test more phone calls but all seems ok. Consider AAWireless is still under development and further improving can arrive in next months but until now all is perfect.

A tip: you can hidden the AAWireless box in the slot on the right side of the gearbox, so it's quite invisible. And you can have Android Auto wireless also on USA models where Abarth internal wifi antenna is poor (AAWireless will always use its wifi access point, not the one on car).

Now, take a minute to check your phone compatibility: AAWireless works with all Android 11 smartphones. It works fine also with Android 10 but there is a problem with Xiaomi/Poco/Redmi: only A1 and A3 work, all other models don't work with AAWireless because strange MIUI behaviour. So if you have a Xiaomi A1 and A3 you are ok (they have a stock Android rom), but if you have another Xiaomi/Poco/Redmi smartphone with Android 10 and you can't update to Android 11, the only way to go is install a custom rom (also Xiaomi.eu roms work). If you have an older Android version this could work but you have to enable developer mode in Android Auto and I don't know if this works fine, check before buying.

I hope I helped angry Android users who want to have an Android Auto wireless experience just 2021 years after Christ born.

@Santtu. European 124 spider have excellent wifi, so you can go with 2) to have AA Wireless, without buying anything and at no cost. That is, you simply hack your firmware without new hardware parts, you get AA Wireless and it's also touch screen enabled. The wifi 124 spider chip is too weak on some 124 spider cars (as USA cars), so in that case AA Wireless will not work or will work poorly.

If you upgrade to fw70 using this guide OR you hack your fw56 and you have a bad wifi chip THEN you can still have AA Wireless buying the AAWireless dongle (all details in my previous post). I only tested it with fw70 but it should work fine also with fw56: it works fine, I'm still using it without any problem, it lost its connection only 1 time and it reconnected automatically after few seconds.

I think this is a useful information because AA Wireless dongle is not so good on all cars and I read nowhere if it's good for our 124 spider with (hacked) firmware / parts, so I bought it and I shared my experience here, so now everyone who wants Android Auto wireless on their cars can have it, buying the dongle. If someone googles for "AAWireless 124 spider" I hope he can reach this thread.

Note: in the last Indiegogo update the developer wrote the chip shortage is causing delays, so if you place your order now you could have it for November (maybe).

I finished this project on Friday, and can report that truly this is the kind of thing that if I can do it, anyone can do it. I did the firmware a couple of weeks again and that took far less time than any of the instructions suggested it might. On the other hand, the hardware installation took me far longer than I've read it has taken for lots of other folks. It took me about 6 hours. But everything came up roses...or Spiders..on the first test!

Today (two days later), as I was out and about, starting and stopping several times, at some stage CarPlay quit working. I could see it in the apps, but it was greyed out and at some stage it said something like "no device recognized." Bluetooth connection worked fine. I came home, locked the car and walked away with the fob (as in the firmware installation), restarted my phone, and when I powered everything up, CarPlay came right back.

Has anyone else had this kind of experience? Overall I am SUPER happy with the whole thing, and am very grateful to @ameridan and @68wooley for their efforts on this.

The Very Best part of the whole process for me was discovering my disconnected Sport Mode cable when I pulled up the center console. I had no idea what I was missing!

Thanks to everyone who has contributed to this project and who has asked and answered questions!
