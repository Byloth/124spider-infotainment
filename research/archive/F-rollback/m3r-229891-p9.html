Thank you all for all the information in this thread. It’s been a live saver for my Mazda CMU.

I’ll summarize my case, maybe it helps anyone.
Starting point: My car, Mazda 6 SW 2016 EU (Spain). Version 56 00 230 EU installed and AIO tweaks installed. I tried to upgrade to latest version to install USB module.
1st mistake: AIO tweaks remained installed. YOU DO always must remove AIO tweaks before updating. They made my failsafe installation fail.
2st mistake: I stop installation midway. YO DO always install failsafe & update (if your version requires both) with your system ON. Don’t switch car off midway.

After 1st and 2nd mistake, my CMU was bricked, screen black and nothing worked ( just the radio….). Panic!
Thanks to this thread and after some struggle I managed to recover it.
PLEASE DO read this thread (more than once) and understand all the procedures. I did it and for me, after a few tries and failures, it worked :
1st.- I reprogrammed my CMU with a USB programmer. I followed video mentioned in post #96 and I used rom rom-513B.bin
2nd after that, I installed cmu150_EU_59.00.502A_reinstall.up

After 1 & 2 my system came back to live! After that, I did all the right things I should have done in the first place. I cleaned my system, removed all tweaks , did a factory reset and procced to a clean install of cmu150_EU_74.00.324A_update.up

Thanks again

Add my few cents worth...

You see in 99% of Cases where you have a Bricked CMU (which is a Black or blank screen and you can ONLY Hear the Radio) is because people find Firmware links in YouTube or Chat or Ai and is totally wrong.

These people offer next to Zero installation instructions and or advice is plain wrong.

Your chances of fixing a bricked CMU are very slim to no chance as you must have the ORIGINAL FIRMWARE which was ORIGINALLY installed at the chinese plant which makes the CMU for PANASONIC and then on to Mazda.

How to find out it's original Firmware?, YOU MUST REMOVE the CMU and look at the big label on the side (usually yellow or white) look for 56.00.513 or 59.00.330, whatever That NUMBER is must be the Firmware you must use.
Recovery of CMU Means pulling it apart and exposing the PCB and connect ribbons to chip RESET then for Firmware install.
Trouble is FINDING the exact Firmware Number.

As ASH8 mentioned the easiest fix for boot-loop or bricked is to buy a CMU from ebay or junkyard.

However, if you are stubborn and don't want to admit defeat you can fix them with chip writing software/hardware, and about 3 months of reading/learning and trial and error (or at least I did cause probably I am slow learner). Now I have a test bench and have a side hobby of refurbishing CMUs, playing around with new tweaks, fixing bad screens, and selling units with latest 74. There has not been a unit (unless physically damaged) that I have not been able to bring back to life.

Bricked units require chip writing as there is no way to fix it as the unit has lost its ability to boot anything (screen, USB, OS, etc).
Bootloops you "could" fix on 56 and lower as 56+ has locked down root password that I don't believe the password been identified in the newer software (yet).

Yes, if you can read the full ROM and write back to it you can assume it's working.

Now i didn't know on the Linux side but take that bin file over to an ASCII reader (i just use Neoprammer for win) and look for text in address 7E0000 you should see something like cmu150_NA_74.....up as example which is the current file ran to update the system. You use address 10000 to flip the bit to start the update process and skip boot of the CMU.

If you see 7e0000 you probably can consider that at l least the bin file wrote good or partly good back to the ROM.

Bin files also can get corrupted and choose a bad write too, i forgot to mention that.
